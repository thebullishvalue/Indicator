How to read the evidence tags

Pragati was built and audited in public, and many of its parts were tested on markets. Not all of them were. Every claim in this manual carries one of three tags.

MeasuredTested in this repository on real market data with a pre-registered plan, and on data the design never saw. The numbers are in docs/trail-lab.md and docs/trail-tradability.md.
ExternalMeasured in the v9 audit (380 instruments, six asset classes, three eras), whose data lives in a separate repository. The numbers are quoted in the script and were not re-checked here.
UnmeasuredA reading aid or a reasoned combination. Useful for context, never proven to make money. Size accordingly.
Part I

Basics

Chapter 1

Getting started

1.1

Setup in five minutes

Add Pragati to the chart.

It opens in its own pane under price, and it draws the trail, the volume profile and the readout table on the price chart itself.

Use standard candles.

On Heikin Ashi, Renko, Kagi, Point & Figure or range bars the prices never traded, so every family pauses and says "non-standard chart".

Start on the daily chart.

Daily bars are what was tested. Weekly bars were tested for the ▲ and the grid. Intraday works, but nothing on it was measured.

Leave the defaults alone at first.

View Full, Trace Conviction × Value, conviction on Ladder down, value on Ladder up, Accepted momentum on. Every number in this manual was measured at these settings.

Give it history.

Conviction's histogram needs about 450 bars to calibrate (1.8 years of daily bars). Until then the push gate is warming: the grid's rows have no memory, the trail cannot start, and the readout says so.

Create one alert.

Choose the condition "Any alert() function call" on Pragati. It carries every event once, at its bar's close (section 13.1).

1.2

Reading a chart in sixty seconds

Five looks, in this order, at the close of a bar. The worked readings come from the readout in Figure 2.2.

Is there a reading?

Anything paused, warming, stale or gold means smaller or later. In Figure 2.2 nothing is gold.

Trader: the AUCTION row's CHART cell.

LONG 0.62: the tested system is long, at size 0.62. Size it with the formula in section 9.1.

Investor: the CVG row's STATE.

Buy 2.71: the TURNED family (buyers in control, price cheap). Weight it at 2.71 units in the book.

Why: the CVG row's WHY and PUSH.

"buyers running · turning up" and ↑: both edges are confirmed and a push is on. Nothing is held.

Context: OI and the auction.

"Long build-up · book 62 pct": new longs, the book not crowded. "Migrating ↑ · trail ↑ · in value": value is moving with the trail. A stop belongs below the trail or the session VAL.

Chapter 2

The visual language

2.1

The colour grammar: one hue, one job

Colours mean the same thing everywhere in Pragati. Learn these six and every element reads itself.

Green · direction up, action buyBuyers in control, a rich value reading, a long position, a Buy or Accumulate action.
Red · direction down, action sellSellers in control, a cheap value reading, a short position, a Trim or Exit action.
Gold · caution, never a directionRead with care: a probe, a quiet regime, a held row, a crowded book, short covering, crypto's capitulation.
Grey · neutralNothing to read, Hold or Wait, Accepted momentum flat.
Blue · the session profile's value areaIdentity, not direction. The VAH, VAL and value rows.
Slate · the trail's rolling value ribbonIdentity: a different value area from the session profile's.
On the value tape, green means rich and red means cheap. A red value tape is a price below fair value, which is what the capitulation ▲ wants.
2.2

The pane, labelled

A column's colour is not who is in control. Control is the trace's side of zero. A green column under a trace at −45 means sellers still control, with their grip easing. When the market is unusually quiet the columns lose half their colour: a loud pane in a quiet market is drawn quiet.

Chapter 3

The engines in plain words

Conviction

How much of the window's effort turned into net movement, weighted by participation (volume, or true range where there is none).

Answers: who is in control? · Seen in: the Conviction trace, the conviction tape, the histogram.

Value (Samanvaya)

Where price stands against fair value: a macro-hedged relative-value leg blended with a breadth leg, in σ. Past θ is cheap or rich.

Answers: is the price stretched? · Seen in: the value tape, the Value trace.

The ladder

Both readings across timeframes. Conviction on Ladder down reads the bars inside each chart bar. Value on Ladder up reads the chart and the frames above.

Answers: is it true on every timeframe? · Seen in: the two tapes.

The Conviction-Value Grid

The two tapes as a 3 × 3 map. Each cell is a family with an action and units, the weights Pragyam sizes from. External

Answers: what should a portfolio hold? · Seen in: the CVG row, the ▲▼, the tint.

The auction

A rolling volume profile of the chart's own bars: where participation was accepted, and whether value is migrating.

Answers: where has the market agreed on price? · Seen in: the trail, the ribbon, the AUCTION row.

Accepted momentum

The six-month acceptance trail sets the side; time-series momentum sets the size. Long only when both say up, short only when both say down. Measured

Answers: should a trend book be long, flat or short? · Seen in: the trail's colour, the AUCTION row's CHART cell.

Open interest

On futures: whether this bar's trading opened risk (builds) or closed it (covering, unwinding). External

Answers: what kind of positioning? · Seen in: the OI row, the gold cast.

Session volume profile

Where volume traded in the current session: POC, value area, VWAP, initial balance, naked POCs. Unmeasured

Answers: where are the reference levels? · Seen in: the right edge of the price chart.

Part II

Reading every state

Chapter 4

The Conviction-Value Grid

Rows come from the conviction tape: UP past the inner zone (+30), DOWN past −30, FAINT between. Columns come from the value tape: CHEAP past −θ, RICH past +θ, FAIR between. Each of the nine cells is a family named for what it says to do. The number is its units, the relative weight a portfolio gives the name.

Rows move only on a push

A row changes only when conviction's own histogram confirms a push that way: on the move's side, not turning, not in a quiet regime. Without that push the row is held where it was, and WHY shows "held" in gold. This stops the grid flickering on every wobble of the tape.

The 5 × 5: phases

Each edge has a momentum that confirms it or not. Conviction confirms a row while buyers (or sellers) are running; it is pausing otherwise. Value confirms a cheap or rich column once it is turning back toward fair; it is still cheapening or still richening otherwise. An unconfirmed edge sits half-way to the middle, so its units fall. Inside a cell the units are also shaded by how strongly each tape is drawn.

Chapter 5

The readout table

Five columns ask the same question on every row. STATE: where the layer stands. WHY: what makes it so. PUSH: what pushes it now. CHART: one more reading that differs by row. SPAN: how long it has stood, counting the current bar as the first. Hover any cell on TradingView for its tooltip.

Chapter 7

Investing with the grid

7.1

The investor's frame

For investing, the grid is the tool. Each name in your universe sits in one family, and its units say how much weight it deserves relative to the others. You hold everything you would hold anyway, more of what is cheap with sellers exhausted, and less of what is rich with sellers in control. Units are relative weights for a book of many names, not a buy-or-sell switch for one.

Evidence External: the graded units were positive in all three eras outside crypto. In Pragyam's allocator the units beat its seed weights in both eras on the Nifty 50 and the Dow 30.

Chapter 8

Plan, book and risk

8.1

The capitulation plan

The one event the v9 audit found positive in every era is the capitulation turn. This is a staged plan around it. The staging itself is a house rule Unmeasured; the state and the turn are External.

1 · Enter DOWN · cheapThe green tint appears; STATE reads Buy 4. Buy the first third.
→
2 · ▲ firesValue momentum turns back toward fair; the tint deepens. Buy the second third.
→
3 · Leaves the cellBASING or FADING (Accumulate 1.5), or TURNED (Buy 3). Buy the last third on the next push ↑.
→
4 · BUILDINGHold 1.5. Let the position run.
→
5 · PAID or STALLINGTrim 0.75.
→
6 · ▼ DISTRIBUTIONExit toward 0.25.

When the plan is wrong

  • Crypto. The capitulation reading failed there. The tint and the ▲ are drawn gold. Skip the plan.
  • Gold readings. A held row, a quiet regime, "basket settling", or a paused grid. Wait for them to clear before adding.
  • A trend against you. If Accepted momentum is SHORT, the long-horizon trend still points down. Keep each tranche small (section 11.1).

8.3

Investor risk

  • Trend filters trade return for drawdown. On 119 markets, holding only while the 20-bar trail was up cut the worst drawdown from −73.5% to −35%, but gave up about 11 points of yearly growth (13.5% against 24.4%) Measured. The 200-day average and plain momentum made the same trade. Accepted momentum was tested as a trading system, not as a long-only filter for an investment book.
  • The ▲ is a lean. About +0.05σ over 10 to 20 bars. It pays across many names and many events, not on any one External.
  • Gold is caution. Reduce or wait; never read gold as a direction.

Chapter 9

Accepted momentum

9.1

The tested system

This is the one trade system in Pragati that passed a held-out test Measured. It was chosen by a fixed rule from eleven trials on data up to 2019, frozen, and then run once on 2020-26 and on 411 markets it had never seen.

Table 9.1 Accepted momentum, the tested rules
RuleExactly
SideThe acceptance trail on a 126-bar (six-month) window: up while value has migrated and ratcheted up, down while it has migrated down
Momentum score SThe 1-, 3-, 6- and 12-month moves of the close (21, 63, 126, 252 bars), each divided by volatility × √horizon, capped at ±2, averaged and halved, so S lies between −1 and +1
PositionSide × max(0, side × S): LONG with size S when both say up, SHORT with size |S| when both say down, FLAT when they disagree
Decide and tradeRead at the close; trade at the next open. Never on the forming bar
SizeWeight = size × 10% ÷ annualised volatility, capped at 2 (two times the market's capital slice)
VolatilityThe test used an exponential average (span 60) of daily log returns, × √252. On TradingView the built-in Historical Volatility indicator with length 60 is a close stand-in Unmeasured
RebalanceOnce a week (every fifth bar in the test). Each market moves to its target only if the target has moved by more than a tenth of itself. That includes going flat and changing sides: between rebalances the position is left alone
When an alert arrives. The position alert fires on the close where the side changes. Under the tested rules you act at the next weekly rebalance. Acting at the next open instead is faster, but that variant was not tested.

Worked sizing example

Book ₹10,00,000 across 20 markets → slice per market ₹50,000 Stock X: readout says LONG 0.62; annualised volatility 28% weight = 0.62 × 10% ÷ 28% = 0.221 notional = 0.221 × ₹50,000 = ₹11,071 price ₹2,500 → buy 4 shares (₹10,000), the nearest whole lot Cap: weight never above 2.0 (notional ₹1,00,000 for this slice)

  • The edge is a portfolio's. On one market the median Sharpe was only +0.23 to +0.38, and about a quarter of markets lost money over the period. Run the same rules on many markets at once.
  • It did not beat plain momentum by the pre-registered test. It matched it at lower turnover and drawdown.
  • Costs. At twice the assumed costs the Sharpe was still +0.69 and +1.11.
  • Risk level, and real drawdowns. At 10% per market the diversified book ran at only about 1.3% to 1.6% volatility, which is why its drawdowns above look small. Scaled to 10% volatility for the whole book, the same paths would have drawn down roughly 16% to 29%. Expect drawdowns of that size at a useful risk level. Getting there needs futures-style leverage, and shorting Indian cash stocks overnight needs stock or index futures.
  • How many markets. The tested books held 119 and 411 markets. With fewer, results drift toward the single-market figures. About 20 to 30 markets across several classes is a practical floor Unmeasured.
  • Where it was weak. The INR currency pairs lost; crypto was about flat (section 11.4).

12.2

When readings disagree

Data first.

Non-standard chart, warming, paused, stale or gold: there is no reading. Do nothing new.

The horizon chooses the tool.

A trading book follows Accepted momentum. An investment book follows the grid's units. Do not let one book borrow the other's signal.

Gold reduces size; it never sets direction.

Held rows, probes, quiet regimes, crowded books and short covering all mean the same thing: smaller, or later.

Do not trade the untested parts.

The 20-bar trail, the ◆, R and H measured null or negative as signals. Read them as structure.

Crypto is the exception.

Skip the capitulation plan there.

Quick reference card

Every close

  • Gold anywhere? Smaller, or later.
  • Trader: read the AUCTION row's CHART cell; act at the next open.
  • Investor: read the CVG row's STATE at the week's last close.

Investor

  • Weight = units ÷ total units.
  • Stage into DOWN · cheap; add on the ▲.
  • Trim in PAID and STALLING; exit in DISTRIBUTION.
  • Not on crypto.

Trader

  • LONG or SHORT only when trail and momentum agree.
  • Weight = size × 10% ÷ volatility, cap 2.
  • Many markets, weekly rebalance.
  • Paper first, then a tenth.

Never

  • Trade the forming bar.
  • Read gold as a direction.
  • Lean on the ◆, R, H or the 20-bar trail.
  • Use non-standard candles.
